# AI for Founders: the co-creation survey

About ten minutes, and most of it is writing, not ticking. We are drafting a sixteen-week programme for founders whose business still runs through them, and before we decide who teaches what, we want to know whether we are teaching the right things. So this asks about your week before it asks about our outline, and it asks you to answer in your own words. Your answers go to the people writing the curriculum, not to a dashboard. Version two is published with the changes marked, so you can see what moved because of you.

## Part 1 · Your week
Before anything about us: where you are, and what it is like. Three quick ones, then one that takes a minute.

**1. Which describes you best right now?**  
_Single choice · required · Acknowledge_
- Solo founder: coach, consultant, practitioner or creator. The business is me.
- Founder with a team of up to ten
- Founder with a team of more than ten
- Planning to start a business in the next year
- I lead inside someone else's company
*Why we ask: The one split that changes everything downstream. Solo founders and founders with a team feel the same problem differently, and every lab runs in two tracks because of it. Sets the wording of question 7.*

**2. What does your business mainly sell?**  
_Single choice · required · Acknowledge_
- Coaching or consulting
- Courses, programmes or digital products
- Agency or done-for-you services
- Software or a product
- Physical products or retail
- Something else (tell us)
*Why we ask: Decides which examples and skill packs we build first. A coach's company brain and an agency's company brain hold different things.*

**3. Tell us about a recent week when everything came back to you. What happened, and how did you feel by Friday?**  
_Open text · required · Acknowledge_
*Why we ask: The first real question names the feeling before we ask for anything. People cannot think about a curriculum while the frustration is unspoken; asked this way, the frustration becomes the data. The phrases here become the opening of the summit and the sales page.*

**4. In a normal week, how many hours does your business need you personally to keep running?**  
_Single choice · required · Acknowledge_
- Under 20
- 20 to 40
- 40 to 60
- More than 60
- I honestly do not know
*Why we ask: The before picture. The whole programme is measured against the founder load map, and this is the first rough reading of it across the community.*

## Part 2 · What you have tried
No judgement here. We learn more from what went wrong than from what went right.

**5. Walk us through the last time you tried to hand something to AI, or to a person. What did you hope would happen, and what actually happened?**  
_Open text · required · Get curious_
*Why we ask: A story, not an opinion. The gap between hoped and happened is the knowledge gap or the application gap, in their words. This is where we learn whether the problem is knowing what to do or getting it to stick.*

**6. What has stopped you going further with AI in your business? Say it the way you would say it to a friend.**  
_Open text · required · Get curious_
*Why we ask: The blocker, uncoded. Asking for the friend version lowers the guard and gets the real reason (trust, time, fear of sounding like a robot, a bad first try) rather than the respectable one.*

**7. Which of these still have to go through you? Tick everything that applies, then tell us the one you would most like off your desk first.**  
_Multiple choice, then one pick · required · Get curious_
- Content and social media
- Finding leads and following them up
- Sales calls and proposals
- Client delivery and client questions
- Admin: inbox, invoices, scheduling
- Hiring, managing and delegating
- The numbers and cash decisions
- Deciding what to build or sell next
*Why we ask: The eight options are the eight modules in plain words and in no particular order. The tick pattern weights the pillars by founder type; the one pick tests the module order. For solo founders the hiring line reads delegating to contractors or AI.*

## Part 3 · The outline
Read the draft outline (shown here and linked) and tell us what you think. Nothing in it is fixed.

**8. For each of the eight modules, how much do you need it?**  
_Grid, one answer per row · required · Get curious_
Rows: Strategy: find the bottleneck; Company brain; Marketing; Sales; Operations and delivery; Team: people and AI; Finance and the founder's cockpit; Product and scale
Scale: Already handled / Nice to have / Need it / Need it urgently
*Why we ask: The one quantitative read on the outline. A four-point scale with no midpoint, so everyone leans one way. The share answering need it or urgently becomes each module's need index, by founder type.*

**9. Reading the outline, where did you think: that is exactly my problem? And where did you think: that is not how it is for me?**  
_Open text · required · Get curious_
*Why we ask: Two-sided on purpose. The first half gives marketing the hooks in the audience's words; the second half tells the curriculum where a lesson is framed for someone else.*

**10. What problem in your business is nowhere in this outline?**  
_Open text · required · Get curious_
*Why we ask: The gap question, asked as a problem rather than a topic. Every answer is logged; the recurring ones become the change log in version two.*

**11. Where would you want us to go deeper, and what would deeper look like for you?**  
_Open text · optional · Get curious_
*Why we ask: Depth, with a definition. Deeper means different things: more time, more hand-holding, a template, a live build on their business. The second half tells us which.*

**12. What would you skip, and why?**  
_Open text · optional · Get curious_
*Why we ask: Permission to cut. Sixteen weeks is long; the why separates already handled from do not believe it works.*

## Part 4 · Who you learn from
Two questions about people. No names are promised; we want to understand what works for you.

**13. Who have you learned the most from about running a business, with AI or without, inside Mindvalley or outside? What is it about how they teach that works for you?**  
_Open text · required · Get curious_
*Why we ask: Vishen's which teachers question, asked open. Names tell marketing which voices this audience already trusts; the second half tells the curriculum what teaching style to brief every guest on. Open, not a list, so nobody is promised and names we have not thought of can surface.*

**14. Is there anyone you would want in the room for this programme? Tell us who, and why them.**  
_Open text · optional · Get curious_
*Why we ask: A wish list, framed as a question rather than a vote. The why matters more than the name: it tells us what the person represents (proof, warmth, a method) so we can look for it in whoever we confirm.*

## Part 5 · Building it together
Now the part that makes this a conversation rather than a form.

**15. If we built this together, what would need to be true in March for you to say the sixteen weeks were worth it?**  
_Open text · required · Build together_
*Why we ask: The promise, in their words and their timeframe. We promise hours back, every lead answered and a business designed around AI; this tells us whether that is what they are buying, and the phrases become the sales page.*

**16. What do you suspect you do not know yet, that a founder running on AI should know?**  
_Open text · optional · Build together_
*Why we ask: Vishen's what they do not know they should know, asked directly. Answers here are the unprogrammed worries, the ones the quiz could not reach because nobody can tick a box for a thing they cannot name.*

**17. Would you like to be part of the founding circle that shapes version two?**  
_Multiple choice · required · Build together_
- Review version two before it is published
- Test one system in my own business and report back
- Share my founder load map, anonymised, as an example
- Be quoted, first name and business type, in the curriculum
- Join a 45-minute group call with the curriculum team
- Not this time, just keep me posted
*Why we ask: The commitment question. Everyone who ticks anything but the last option has put something in, and the list becomes the founding circle: the people most likely to enrol and most likely to tell others.*

**18. Anything else you want the people building this to know?**  
_Open text · optional · Build together_
*Why we ask: The door left open. The most useful answer in a survey is often to the question nobody asked.*

**19. May we follow up with you on your answers?**  
_Yes or no, with email pre-filled · required · Build together_
- Yes
- No, use my answers but do not contact me about them
*Why we ask: Consent, and the join to the quiz and summit records. Pre-filling the email from the campaign link removes the one field that costs completions.*

## How this answers the 6 October brief

- **Where they are.** Part one: who they are, what they sell, how many hours the business needs them, and a week told in their own words (questions 1 to 4).
- **How they want to grow.** Question 15, what would need to be true in March for sixteen weeks to have been worth it, and question 9, where the outline is exactly their problem and where it is not.
- **What they want changed.** Questions 9 to 12 on the outline: what is exactly right, what is missing, where to go deeper, what to skip. Every answer is logged and the recurring ones become the version two change log.
- **Biggest needs.** Question 3 (the week), question 7 (what still goes through them and which to lift first) and the module need grid at question 8.
- **What they do not know they should know.** Asked directly at question 16, and surfaced indirectly by questions 5 and 6, where the story of the last attempt shows the gap they cannot name.
- **Which teachers.** Questions 13 and 14, open: who they have learned most from, inside Mindvalley or outside, what it is about how that person teaches, and who they would want in the room. No list of names, so nothing is promised and names we have not thought of can appear.
- **Serves marketing as well as curriculum.** Question 9 and question 15 give the language for the summit and the sales page in the audience's own words; question 13 tells marketing which voices this audience already trusts; question 17 builds the founding circle list.
- **A reusable model.** The five parts are programme-agnostic: your week, what you have tried, the outline, who you learn from, building it together. Swap the module list and the outline link and it runs for any Mastery. Jaideep's deeper quiz at summit registration stays separate; this one runs before, to the warm community.

## Design notes

- Length: about ten minutes. Nineteen questions, twelve of them open; nine required. The intro says answer the ones that speak to you, so a half-finished survey still gives us parts one and two.
- Order follows Compassionate Curiosity: acknowledge (part 1), get curious (parts 2 to 4), build together (part 5). The feeling is asked for before any opinion about us; the commitment comes last, once they have already invested.
- Open questions ask for a story or a specific, never a rating in words: a recent week, the last time you tried, what would deeper look like, what would need to be true in March. Specifics are quotable; opinions are not.
- Closed questions do two jobs only: segment (1, 2, 4) and give one quantitative read on the outline (7, 8). Everything else is written in their words.
- No leading wording, no agree/disagree statements, one idea per question. The four-point scale has no midpoint.
- Branching: question 1 sets the wording of question 7. The outline is shown inline before question 8 so nobody leaves the form.
- Incentive is recognition, not a prize: early sight of version two, a named place in the thank-you section, the founding circle.
- Send on a Tuesday, remind on Friday, close the following Tuesday. One reminder, to non-openers and partial completions only.
- Mobile first, progress shown, email pre-filled from the campaign link. Typeform or an Airtable form; responses land in the AI for Founders base once Vishen has aligned, in a sheet until then.
- No question about the time slot: it is set at 7am Pacific for team capacity, and asking would imply a choice people do not have. The invitation says live when you can, recording when you cannot.

## What we do with the answers

- Segment every answer by founder type (question 1) and what they sell (question 2). The draft already runs two tracks; this tells us whether two is enough.
- Open answers are read in full, not summarised. Questions 3, 5, 6, 9, 15 and 16 are coded by hand into recurring themes; the phrases themselves go into a language bank for lesson copy and the sales page.
- Module need index: the share answering need it or need it urgently on question 8, by segment, read against question 7 to test the module order.
- Gaps and depth: questions 10 to 12 become the version two change log, with a rough count beside each change.
- Teachers: questions 13 and 14 produce two lists, names and teaching qualities. The qualities brief every guest; the names go to the summit and Mastery roster conversation, not to the page.
- Format: the slot is set at 7am Pacific for team capacity; people are encouraged to be live and to watch the recording when they cannot. No question asks about it, because they have no control over it.
- Founding circle: everyone who ticks an active option in question 17 goes on a list with their answers attached. They see version two first.
- Version two is published with a change log that credits the survey: what moved, and roughly how many people asked for it.
