# AI for Founders: the co-creation survey

Eight minutes. Twenty questions and a consent line; five are optional. We are drafting a sixteen-week programme for founders whose business still runs through them, and before we decide who teaches what, we want to know whether we are teaching the right things. Your answers go to the people writing the curriculum, not to a dashboard. Version two is published with the changes marked, so you can see what moved because of you.

## Your business
Four quick ones so we can read the rest of your answers in context.

## Design notes

- Length: about eight minutes. Twenty questions plus the consent line; five are optional (revenue, and four of the open questions). Everything optional is marked so.
- Order: easy facts first (1 to 4), then the big open question (8) while energy is high, the outline grid in the middle, commitment at the end. The contact question is last and pre-filled.
- One idea per question. No double-barrelled questions, no leading wording, no agree/disagree statements. Scales have four points and no midpoint, so everyone leans one way.
- Open questions ask for a story or a specific, never an opinion: the last task you did this week, name a lesson, a year from now. Specifics are quotable; opinions are not.
- Branching: question 1 sets the wording of question 6 (solo founders see delegating to contractors or AI in place of hiring and managing). Question 7 lists only what was ticked in 6.
- The outline is linked from the survey and shown inline before question 10, so nobody has to leave the form to answer.
- Incentive is recognition, not a prize: early sight of version two, a named place in the thank-you section, the founding circle. Warm leads respond to being taken seriously.
- Send on a Tuesday, remind on Friday, close the following Tuesday. One reminder only, to non-openers and partial completions.
- Mobile first, progress shown, email pre-filled from the campaign link. Typeform or an Airtable form; responses land in the AI for Founders base once Vishen has aligned, in a sheet until then.

## What we do with the answers

- Segment every answer by founder type (question 1) and revenue band (question 3). The draft already runs two tracks; this tells us whether two is enough.
- Module need index: the share answering need it or need it urgently on question 10, by segment. Read against questions 7 and 11 to test the module order.
- Blocker map: question 9 counts against the module each blocker maps to. Where a blocker is common and its module is late in the programme, we move the fix earlier or into Get AI-Ready.
- Language mining: questions 8, 12 and 18 are read in full, not summarised. Recurring phrases become lesson language and sales-page copy; recurring problems become the version two change log.
- Format: questions 16 and 17 set lab design and give the time-slot decision a second source alongside the attendance data.
- Founding circle: everyone who ticks an active option in question 19 goes on a list with their answers attached. They see version two first.
- Version two is published with a change log that credits the survey: what moved, and roughly how many people asked for it.
