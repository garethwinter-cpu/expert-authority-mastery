The argument in 30 seconds

The programme has already been sold. This is what goes inside it.

At Mindvalley U, Vishen ran a six-hour accelerator, named the four-month Mastery from the stage, priced it, and took applications. Six hours cannot carry 22 years. This document is the road the compass pointed at, and, per our own non-negotiable rule, the curriculum that must exist before a single contract goes out.

S

Situation

Vishen delivered Become a World-Class Expert and Authority in Your Niche: six hours, ~600 in the room, thousands streaming, five guest specialists. From the stage he named the next product: a four-month Mastery, primarily taught by him, to build the student's positioning, brand and product step ladder.

C

Complication

Six hours is a compass, not a road, and Vishen said so on his own stage. Webinars alone are "about 10 hours." Instagram is "20, 30 hours to master." Students left with direction and no architecture. Meanwhile the Mastermind above it carries a hard $100K floor, so the people who most need the road have nowhere to walk.

Reason 04 · The format, and its failure modes

Our own cohorts already told us what to copy and what to fix

What the data rewards

Paired Teach → build day holds the room

Speaking & Influence Mastery redesigned into a twice-weekly paired cadence for 2026. By session 11, the 2025 cohort had collapsed to 31.3% live attendance. The 2026 cohort is at 57.7%, and the last-three-session average moved from 41.5% to 56.4%, +14.8 points. Detractor rate fell from 4.4% to 3.3%.

AI Mastery's Implementation Labs (build sessions, not discussions) rate 9.0 to 10.0, including a perfect 10 for a hands-on build.

What the data punishes

Abstract openers, unstructured rooms, improvised second sessions

AIM 2026's opener, "Kick Off & Define Your Vision in the Age of AI", scored 6.04, the lowest in its base, in front of 661 people. Unstructured community calls: 5.89.

And the warning closest to home: S&I 2026's workshops rate 9.25 against 9.55 for its teaching lessons. Every 2026 workshop (9.11, 9.12, 9.35, 9.41) sits below every teaching lesson bar the opener. That gap is why the build day gets its own specialist owner here rather than the teacher running a part two.

Design rule 01

Open with a build

Week 1 is not a visioning session. Every student publishes a video before the first week ends. The 6.04 opener is the counter-example we design against.

Design rule 02

Every lab day has a named owner and ships an artefact

No lab day ends in discussion, and none is improvised by the Tuesday teacher. Each has a specialist owner and closes with a named deliverable in the student's hands.

Design rule 03

Every community touchpoint has a job

Pods of 6–12, hot seats, a wins wall, a weekly numbers review. No open hangouts: 5.89 is what those score.

Build one rung. Build the thing that sells it. Turn on the tap. Then climb.

The three overlap rules

Content: AI for Founders automates the pipeline; Expert & Authority finds the voice that goes into it. Offers: AI for Founders prices and delivers what exists; Expert & Authority designs the ladder that doesn't exist yet. Demand: AI for Founders builds the capture mechanism; Expert & Authority builds the reason anyone fills it in. Neither programme teaches the other's half, and the zero-overlap faculty enforces it.

Why the boundary is a retention mechanism

S&I's own detractors flagged scope drift, verbatim: "I'm only interested in speaking, not creating a course or teaching or creating a business" — on the week that programme drifted into Expert & Authority territory. Scope drift is not a marketing problem; it is a refund generator. Each programme teaches its own promise and cross-refers the rest. The sequence also matches our intake: 68% female, 78% aged 45+, service businesses built on expertise, and 91% of Social Media Mastery students not yet earning from their audience. Those students have nothing to systematise yet.
