The language we use

One fixes demand. One fixes capacity.
Here's what that actually means.

Every struggling expert business is broken in one of two places. Either not enough people want to buy from you (a demand problem), or plenty do and you can't serve them without breaking yourself (a capacity problem). The programmes split exactly on that line.

Demand → Expert & Authority

A demand problem sounds like this

  • "I'm great at what I do, but nobody knows me."
  • "Clients only come from referrals — and referrals are drying up."
  • "I post, and nothing happens. I'm invisible next to people half as good as me."
  • "I undercharge, because nobody knows why I'm worth more."
  • "I have no list, no product, no pipeline — just me, hoping the phone rings."

Fixing demand means building the reasons people come to you: a name they recognise, a story they remember, an offer they can buy today and a bigger one behind it. You graduate with people arriving, and paying.

Capacity → AI for Founders

A capacity problem sounds like this

  • "My business can't run a single day without me."
  • "Sales are fine — it's delivery that's killing me. Every new client adds hours to my week."
  • "Leads go cold because I'm too busy serving the customers I already have."
  • "If I take a week off, the business takes a week off."
  • "Growing means hiring, and I don't want the headcount — or the management."

Fixing capacity means the business no longer needing you for everything: marketing, follow-up, delivery and reporting running as systems, with you doing only what only a founder can. You graduate with a machine, and your Fridays.

The differentiators

Two pathways, opposite problems

One question routes any prospect: "Do you have something to sell that's drowning you, or something to say that nobody's paying for yet?" Drowning in delivery → AI for Founders. Unknown with real expertise → Expert & Authority.
