The problem we are solving together

One customer, one promise, one business case. Three drafts of the same answer.

This page is not the team's case against Vishen's draft, or Vishen's against the team's. It is the three of us (the team, Vishen, and the evidence) against one problem: how do we build the best curriculum, experience and learning journey for the people who have actually registered, and make it a business that keeps them, refunds few of them, and leaves them better off? Every row below is written to be read by everyone who has to sign it.

The customer, measured

626 registrants by 8 September

Experienced and unseen. 76% earn under $25K from their expertise (53% nothing yet); 10% are at $100K or more. 66% say nobody knows they exist or they cannot put what they do into words. 69% pick "The Expert"; 35% have been at it more than ten years. Asked what success looks like: a product line that sells without trading hours (27%), fully booked at premium rates (25%), THE name in their niche (21%), first paying clients (20%).

Summit thank-you quiz, Airtable, 527 to 564 answers per question. Same direction as the 36 answers of 7 September.

The promise

Be chosen: a name, a product, a ladder

Vishen named it from the stage: four months, $4,999, primarily taught by him, to build "your position, your brand, your product step ladder." The quiz says the room wants exactly that: a named place in their niche and a product line that earns. Both drafts agree on the destination; the comparison is about the road.

Accelerator transcript, MVU 2026; quiz success question, n=527.

The business

High retention, low refunds, an experience people talk about

The nearest precedent is a warning: AI Mastery 2026 finished at +84 NPS and still refunded 23.8%, with 40.7% on deposits, because onboarding was reactive. Speaking & Influence held 56% late-programme attendance on a paired cadence where the previous cohort fell to 31%. The speaker budget line is 15% of programme revenue. The design has to earn its retention in the first fourteen days, not at the close.

AIM 2026 dashboard; S&I 2025–26 attendance base; 1 Sep budget line. Detail in the Retention & the money section.

The recommendation

Build Version 3. Take it to Vishen as the answer to his documents, not a substitute for them.

Version 1 is the safer build but it was written before Vishen's documents, and its summit sells the way the lowest-rated closes did (a 2023-style hard pitch at 3.78 against a 2025-style integration close at 4.53). Version 2 has the best summit arc and the sharpest identity thinking of the three, and it is not deliverable as written: sixteen weeks with no teacher, five weeks aimed at a customer who is one person in thirty-six, a price and fees that do not exist yet, and two documents that disagree on which week builds what. Version 3 keeps everything from Version 2 that the evidence supports and everything from Version 1 that makes it deliverable. It holds on seven of eight criteria; the eighth is a single open number, Priestley's fee, which resolves either way with a fallback already scheduled.

Choose Version 1 only if the decision is to set Vishen's documents aside entirely, which nobody has proposed. Choose Version 2 only if the room changes: if the quiz re-read before the summit shows Founders and Scalers outnumbering Experts, his Weeks 8, 15 and 17 come back into scope and the argument re-opens. Otherwise, Version 3.
