Seven insights

What the answers say when you cross them

Single counts describe the room. Crossing them describes the rooms inside it, and that is what a curriculum has to be built for.

Insight 1

Tenure does not buy revenue. A decade in, a third have still never sold.

Of the 2,359 people with more than ten years’ experience, 788 have never sold anything from it. Only the under-one-year group is meaningfully greener. This is the whole thesis of the programme in one row: expertise is not the bottleneck, the machine around it is.

Insight 2

Four types, and they are genuinely different rooms.

Creators are the least monetised (68% never sold) and the most likely to say they cannot put what they do into words (26%). Founders are the most likely to have followers who do not buy (22%). Established Experts are a different species: 63% earn six figures or more and 41% name the plateau, not invisibility, as the problem.

Insight 3

The pain moves with the money, in a predictable order.

At zero revenue, 61% say nobody knows they exist. Under $25K, “people follow me but don’t buy” jumps to 26%, its peak. From $100K up, the plateau takes over and reaches 46% above $1M. That is the same left-to-right walk as the curriculum: Be Seen, then Architecture, then Show Up and stay chosen. The sequence is right. The question is whether each stage is weighted for the people actually in it.

Insight 4

Success moves too: first clients, then fully booked, then a product line, then the name.

A third of the never-sold want their first paying client. By $25K that collapses to 3% and “fully booked at premium rates” peaks at 36%. From $100K the answer becomes the product line and the name, and above $1M one in eight wants the room of peers, more than double any other band. The Guild is the right answer for that top band, and the quiz already routes them there.

Insight 5

In their own words, the biggest ask is a system they can follow.

5,582 people answered the open question. Two in five ask, in some form, for the how: a roadmap, a structure, a step-by-step, someone to tell them where to start. A third talk about selling, pricing and making an offer. A quarter talk about being seen. Only 6% name fear, even though 48% chose invisibility as the thing that breaks. People describe the symptom, not the wound.

“Giving a blueprint or system from start to finish like a dummies version.”

“I know I can but I don’t know how to structure a product, an offer, and how to have automatic sales.”

Recommendations

How I would strengthen the curriculum from this

Nine moves, each tied to the evidence above and to the week it lands in. None of them changes the spine. The sequence the quiz describes is the sequence Version 3 already teaches.

W17

Make the Authority Blueprint the weekly tracker, not a summit slide.

The single largest ask in the open answers is a system to follow, start to finish. The summit shows thirteen Blueprint pieces on Day 1 and promises them finished in the Mastery. Hand every student a one-page Blueprint on Day 1 of Week 1 with the thirteen pieces and the week each is built, and open every Tuesday by marking the next one. It costs one slide a week and answers the question two in five people asked.

Evidence · 2,246 open answers (40%) ask for the how · “where to start” and “blueprint” named outright by 125

Give pricing and the ask a full clinic, not a lab afternoon.

Pricing and charging appear in the open answers as often as fear, courage and self-doubt combined. “Fully booked at premium rates” is the second most chosen definition of success and peaks in exactly the band that has started selling and stalled. Ajit’s pricing hour on the Week 14 build day should become a named pricing clinic with a price set and defended out loud by every student, and the Week 15 enrolment conversation should end with each student having asked one real person for real money, on tape.

Name leverage as an outcome and audit for it.

W1

Put the one-sentence exercise in Week 1, before the first post.

Give the Established Experts their own thread, and the Guild bridge earlier.

Signpost the book and the stage, without teaching them.

One open answer in seven mentions a book, speaking, a stage or a podcast. That is Speaking & Influence territory and the three-programme boundary should hold. But the room is telling us where it wants to be known, so the Week 16 Get Booked thread should say so plainly: your book and your talk are rungs on this ladder, here is where they sit, and here is the programme that builds them. A boundary the student can see is a referral. One they cannot see is a disappointment.

Do not add a mindset week. The room named symptoms, and Weeks 1 and 2 already treat the wound.

Data hygiene

Three things to fix in the quiz itself

Browser-translated answers split the counts

151 responses arrived with the option text in Spanish, Portuguese, German, Italian, Polish, Russian and a dozen others, because the quiz was submitted through a translated browser. The tenure question alone produced forty variant strings. Every figure on this page is normalised, but the Airtable is not. Lock the option values at ingest or map them in a formula field.

Q1 changed mid-flight

447 people answered the original “which sounds most like you” before it was replaced with the four types, and 605 more answered neither. The four-type question is answered by 86% of the table. If the types drive routing, the old answers need mapping or the routing has a blind spot.

The type question under-reads the top of the room

368 people earning six figures or more still chose “The Expert”. Revenue is the more reliable signal for routing to the Guild and for podding Established Experts in the Mastery. Treat Q3 as the primary fork and Q1 as flavour.
